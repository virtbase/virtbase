## Preamble

On the basis of the [General Terms and Conditions](/en/legal/terms) (hereinafter "Main Contract"), Virtbase provides the Customer with virtual servers (VPS) and related services. If the Customer stores or processes personal data on these servers for which it is the controller within the meaning of Art. 4(7) GDPR, Virtbase processes this data on its behalf. This agreement sets out the parties' data protection obligations. It applies only insofar as the Customer is a business within the meaning of § 14 BGB, a legal person under public law or another controller that does not process personal data exclusively in the course of a purely personal or household activity.

## § 1 Subject matter, duration and scope

1. The subject matter of this agreement is the processing of personal data by Virtbase on behalf of the Customer in providing the services under the Main Contract. The nature, purpose and scope of the processing, the type of data and the categories of data subjects are set out in **Annex 1**.
2. The term of this agreement follows the term of the Main Contract. Obligations that by their nature continue after the end of the contract (in particular § 9 and § 12) remain in force as long as Virtbase processes the Customer's data.
3. This agreement does not cover the processing of the Customer's own master, contract, billing and usage data (e.g. name, address, email address, payment data, login data for the customer portal), which Virtbase processes as an independent controller. Virtbase's [privacy policy](/en/legal/privacy) applies to this.
4. This agreement also does not cover the handling of abuse reports, requests for information from authorities and notices under Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (Digital Services Act) and Regulation (EU) 2021/784 (TCO Regulation). In these cases, Virtbase acts as controller in fulfilment of its own legal obligations or legitimate interests.

## § 2 Nature of the service

1. Virtbase provides an infrastructure service (Infrastructure as a Service). The Customer receives administrative access to its virtual server and decides alone which software it runs and which data it processes there.
2. As a rule, Virtbase takes no notice of content on the Customer's servers. Content is only accessed insofar as this is necessary to provide the service (e.g. creating and restoring backups, reinstallation, migration), to remedy faults, on the Customer's express instruction or because of a legal obligation.

## § 3 Instructions

1. Virtbase processes personal data only on documented instructions from the Customer, including with regard to transfers to a third country, unless Virtbase is required to do so by Union or Member State law. In such a case, Virtbase informs the Customer of that legal requirement before processing, unless that law prohibits such information on important grounds of public interest (Art. 28(3)(a) GDPR).
2. The instructions are initially set out in the Main Contract and this agreement. All actions that the Customer itself triggers via the customer portal, the API or the Discord bot (e.g. creating, reinstalling, backing up or deleting a server) also count as instructions.
3. The Customer gives individual instructions in text form to [support@virtbase.com](mailto:support@virtbase.com). The Customer confirms oral instructions in text form without undue delay.
4. If Virtbase considers that an instruction infringes data protection provisions, Virtbase informs the Customer without undue delay (Art. 28(3), third sentence, GDPR). Virtbase may suspend the execution of the instruction until the Customer confirms or changes it.

## § 4 Confidentiality

Virtbase ensures that all persons authorised to process the data have committed themselves to confidentiality or are under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality (Art. 28(3)(b) GDPR). The obligation continues after the end of their activity.

## § 5 Technical and organisational measures

1. Virtbase takes all technical and organisational measures required under Art. 32 GDPR. The measures applicable at the conclusion of the contract are described in **Annex 3**.
2. The measures are subject to technical progress. Virtbase may replace them with equivalent or better measures, provided the level of protection is not reduced. Significant changes are documented.
3. The Customer is itself responsible for the security of the systems and data within its virtual server, in particular for operating system and software updates, access credentials, firewall configuration within the server, encryption of application data and its own backups.

## § 6 Sub-processors

1. The Customer gives Virtbase general authorisation to engage other processors (sub-processors). The sub-processors engaged at the conclusion of the contract are listed in **Annex 2** and are deemed approved.
2. Virtbase informs the Customer at least 30 days before engaging or replacing a sub-processor by email to the address stored in the customer account. The Customer may object to the change within this period in text form for an important reason relating to data protection. If no objection is made in time, the change is deemed approved. If the Customer objects and the parties cannot agree, both parties may cancel the affected services with 14 days' notice.
3. Virtbase contractually binds each sub-processor to data protection obligations that essentially correspond to those of this agreement (Art. 28(4) GDPR). Virtbase is liable to the Customer for the sub-processor's compliance with these obligations.
4. Ancillary services that Virtbase obtains from third parties and that do not involve access to the Customer's personal data, such as pure transport and telecommunications services, do not count as sub-processing. In these cases too, Virtbase ensures the protection of the data through appropriate measures.

## § 7 Place of processing and transfers to third countries

1. The data on the Customer's virtual servers, including backups created with the backup function, is stored and processed exclusively in the data centre in Eygelshoven (the Netherlands) and thus within the European Union.
2. A transfer to a third country only takes place if the requirements of Art. 44 et seq. GDPR are met and, insofar as data on the Customer's servers is concerned, only on the Customer's documented instruction.
3. If the Customer uses the AI firewall assistant, the configuration data of its server required for this is transferred to the providers in the USA named in Annex 2, section B. Triggering the feature in the customer portal counts as an instruction within the meaning of § 3.

## § 8 Assistance

1. Virtbase assists the Customer with appropriate technical and organisational measures in fulfilling the rights of data subjects under Chapter III GDPR (Art. 28(3)(e) GDPR). As the Customer itself has full access to its servers, it can as a rule implement access, rectification, erasure and data portability itself.
2. If a data subject contacts Virtbase directly, Virtbase forwards the request to the Customer without undue delay, provided it can be attributed to the Customer. Virtbase does not answer such requests itself unless the Customer instructs it to.
3. Taking into account the information available to it, Virtbase assists the Customer in complying with the obligations under Art. 32 to 36 GDPR, in particular with data protection impact assessments and prior consultation of the supervisory authority (Art. 28(3)(f) GDPR).
4. For assistance that goes beyond the cooperation owed by law or that was caused by the Customer's misconduct, Virtbase may charge a reasonable fee.

## § 9 Notification of personal data breaches

1. Virtbase notifies the Customer of any personal data breach affecting the Customer's data that comes to its knowledge without undue delay, and in any event within 48 hours of becoming aware of it. The notification is sent by email to the address stored in the customer account.
2. Insofar as known, the notification contains the information under Art. 33(3) GDPR, in particular the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, the likely consequences and the measures taken or proposed. Information not yet available at the time of the initial notification is provided without undue delay.
3. Virtbase takes the necessary measures to secure the data and mitigate possible adverse effects without undue delay and coordinates them with the Customer as far as the situation allows.
4. Notifying the supervisory authority and communicating the breach to data subjects are the Customer's responsibility.

## § 10 Evidence and audits

1. On request, Virtbase makes available to the Customer all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with the obligations under Art. 28 GDPR (Art. 28(3)(h) GDPR). Evidence can be provided in particular through documentation of the technical and organisational measures, self-assessments and certificates or audit reports of sub-processors.
2. The Customer may carry out audits, including inspections, itself or through an auditor it has engaged who is bound to confidentiality and is not a competitor of Virtbase. Inspections must be announced at least 30 days in advance, take place during normal business hours and must not disrupt operations disproportionately. Audits for a specific reason, in particular after a personal data breach, are possible at a reasonably shorter notice.
3. As the infrastructure is located in a third-party data centre, Virtbase can only provide access to premises within the rules of the data centre operator. Inspections must not endanger the security and confidentiality of other customers' data.
4. The Customer bears its own costs of audits. For on-site audits, Virtbase may charge reasonable compensation for its effort, unless the audit reveals a breach of this agreement by Virtbase.

## § 11 Customer obligations

1. The Customer is solely responsible for the lawfulness of the processing, including the permissibility of the processing on its behalf, and for safeguarding the rights of data subjects.
2. The Customer informs Virtbase without undue delay if it discovers errors or irregularities relating to data protection provisions when reviewing the services.
3. The Customer only processes special categories of personal data (Art. 9 GDPR) or data relating to criminal convictions (Art. 10 GDPR) on the servers after examining itself whether the measures described in Annex 3 are sufficient for its protection needs, and supplements them with its own measures where necessary (e.g. encryption).

## § 12 Deletion and return at the end of the contract

1. After the end of the services, the Customer has the opportunity to back up its data itself until the end of the contract term. Virtbase then deletes all of the Customer's data on the virtual servers, including backups created with the backup function, unless Union or Member State law requires storage of the data (Art. 28(3)(g) GDPR).
2. A server that has been deactivated after its term expired is permanently deleted 5 days after deactivation (§ 18 of the Main Contract). Virtbase points this out to the Customer by email at deactivation.
3. On request, Virtbase confirms the deletion to the Customer in text form.

## § 13 Liability

Art. 82 GDPR applies to the parties' liability. Otherwise, the liability provisions of the Main Contract apply, unless mandatory law provides otherwise.

## § 14 Final provisions

1. This agreement is concluded with the acceptance of the terms when ordering (§ 17(2) of the Main Contract). It is available at [virtbase.com/en/legal/dpa](/en/legal/dpa) and can be downloaded there as a PDF. It is in writing; electronic form is sufficient (Art. 28(9) GDPR).
2. In the event of contradictions between this agreement and the Main Contract, the provisions of this agreement prevail insofar as the protection of personal data is concerned.
3. Virtbase communicates changes to this agreement to the Customer in text form at least 30 days before they take effect. If the Customer does not object within this period, the changes are deemed accepted. Virtbase will expressly point out this consequence in the notice. Changes that are required by mandatory law apply from their effective date.
4. The law of the Federal Republic of Germany applies. The place of jurisdiction is, where permissible, Chemnitz (§ 22(2) of the Main Contract).
5. Should individual provisions of this agreement be or become invalid, this does not affect the validity of the remaining provisions.

## Annex 3 – Technical and organisational measures

### Preliminary remark

Virtbase provides unmanaged virtual servers. The security of the systems within a virtual server (operating system, applications, access credentials, firewall in the guest system, encryption of application data, own backups) is the Customer's responsibility. The following measures concern the infrastructure and management platform operated by Virtbase.

Virtbase is a small company. Only two administrators, known by name, have access to the infrastructure. There are no employees or external service providers with system access.

### 1. Confidentiality (Art. 32(1)(b) GDPR)

#### 1.1 Physical access control

Objective: to deny unauthorised persons access to data processing facilities.

- All servers are located in a single data centre: SkyLink Data Center, Eygelshoven (the Netherlands).
- Access to the building is controlled and monitored. It is regulated by the operator of the data centre.
- Remote hands services of the data centre are not used, or only without access to data.
- The servers are protected against tampering in the event of physical access by boot protection (TPM in combination with a BIOS password).
- The administrators' workplaces are located in private rooms that are not accessible to the public.

#### 1.2 System access control

Objective: to prevent the use of the systems by unauthorised persons.

- A console server (out-of-band) provides emergency access to the infrastructure independently of the production network.
- Administrative access to servers and the virtualisation platform (Proxmox) exclusively via SSH with key authentication. Password logins are disabled.
- Two-factor authentication for administrative access.
- Access credentials are managed exclusively in an encrypted password manager (Proton Pass).
- The administrators' work devices are fully encrypted (LUKS on Linux, BitLocker on Windows, each AES-XTS-256).
- Customer portal: passwords are only stored as a hash. Customers can use two-factor authentication (TOTP with backup codes) and passkeys. Sessions expire after 3 days. Failed login attempts are limited.
- API access only with personal API keys, which can be revoked individually and are subject to rate limiting.

#### 1.3 Data access control

Objective: to ensure that authorised persons only access the data they are authorised to access.

- Customers only have access to their own servers and data. Authorisation is checked on the server side for every request.
- The administration console is only accessible to accounts with the administrator role. Only the managing director has this role. The second administrator only has access to the infrastructure (hypervisor, storage, network), not to the customer portal, administration console or database.
- Virtbase only accesses the content of customer servers insofar as this is necessary to provide the service, to remedy faults, on the Customer's instruction or because of a legal obligation.
- Virtbase does not store a server's root password permanently.
- Credentials for connected third-party services are stored encrypted with AES-256-GCM, with a separate key for each integration.

#### 1.4 Separation control

Objective: to process data collected for different purposes separately.

- Each customer server is a separate virtual machine (KVM) with its own virtual disk. Isolation is provided by the hypervisor.
- Network separation of the customer servers through firewall rules at the level of the virtualisation platform.
- The production, test and development environments of the management platform are separate.
- Data of the management platform (customer accounts, orders) is stored separately from the data on the customer servers.

#### 1.5 Pseudonymisation and encryption (Art. 32(1)(a) GDPR)

- All storage media in the storage cluster (Ceph) are encrypted with LUKS2.
- Connections to the customer portal, to the API and between the platform components are encrypted with TLS.
- Data exports for customers (Art. 15, 20 GDPR) are encrypted with a randomly generated passphrase and deleted after 7 days.
- When an account is deleted, billing and accounting data that must be retained for tax law reasons is anonymised instead of deleted.

### 2. Integrity (Art. 32(1)(b) GDPR)

#### 2.1 Transfer control

Objective: to protect data against unauthorised reading, copying or alteration during transmission and transport.

- Transmission exclusively over encrypted connections (TLS, SSH).
- Data is only passed on to third parties to the sub-processors named in Annex 2 or because of a legal obligation.
- Decommissioned storage media are securely erased or physically destroyed before disposal. As all storage media are encrypted, the data cannot be read without the key.

#### 2.2 Input control

Objective: to be able to trace who entered, changed or deleted which data and when.

- Orders, status changes of orders and subscriptions, and payments are logged with a timestamp and the triggering party.
- Measures in abuse cases (throttling, isolation, shutdown) are stored with time, reason and previous state.
- Login sessions are stored with IP address and browser identifier and can be viewed and revoked by the Customer.
- Platform errors are recorded in a self-operated error tracking system (Sentry). Default personal data is not transmitted.

### 3. Availability and resilience (Art. 32(1)(b) GDPR)

- Each rack is connected to two independent power circuits (A/B), and each server has two power supplies.
- The data centre is built to Tier 3 standard (maintenance of power and cooling possible without interruption) and has redundant fibre connections.
- Incoming network traffic is permanently filtered by DDoS protection (aurologic).
- The data is stored in a distributed storage system (Ceph).
- Monitoring of the infrastructure with automatic alerting (Prometheus, Alertmanager).
- Abuse by individual customers (e.g. outgoing attacks) is detected and can be restricted in stages to protect availability for other customers.

### 4. Recoverability (Art. 32(1)(c) GDPR)

- Customers can create and restore backups of their servers via the customer portal. The backups are stored in the same data centre.
- Note: backups are stored at the same location as the servers. They protect against operating errors and software errors, not against the failure of the entire data centre. Customers are obliged to back up important data additionally at another location.
- The database of the management platform is backed up by the provider (Neon, Frankfurt region) with point-in-time recovery.

### 5. Procedure for regular testing, assessment and evaluation (Art. 32(1)(d) GDPR)

- These measures are reviewed and updated at least annually and when there is a reason to do so (e.g. after security incidents or significant technical changes).
- Security updates for the hypervisor, operating systems and platform software are applied promptly.
- The source code of the management platform is public (AGPL-3.0) and can be reviewed by third parties. Among other things, automated tests check that all personal data is fully covered by export and deletion.
- A process for notifying customers of personal data breaches within 48 hours has been established.
- All persons with access have committed themselves to confidentiality in writing.

### 6. Control of processing on behalf

- Sub-processors are carefully selected and contractually bound under Art. 28(4) GDPR.
- The Customer's instructions are received and documented in text form.
