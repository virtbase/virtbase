## Why the Netherlands

Eygelshoven sits on the Dutch side of the border, a short drive from Aachen.
German traffic barely leaves the region, and Amsterdam and Frankfurt, where most
European networks hand traffic to each other, are both close by.

Your data also stays inside the EU and under the GDPR. If you are in Germany,
Austria or Switzerland, you get latency that feels local without your data
leaving European law.

## Power

Tier 3 is a design standard: the power and cooling can be worked on while
customer equipment keeps running.

Each rack is fed twice, from an A side and a B side, and the two never meet
before the server. Our hosts have a power supply on each. If a feed drops for
maintenance or because a PDU fails, the machine carries on running from the
other one.

![Two independent power feeds, A and B, each reaching a separate power supply in the same host system.](/assets/static/datacenter/power-path-en.svg)

*Either feed on its own carries the full load.*

## Cooling

Chilled air is pushed into a closed aisle between two rows of racks. Because the
aisle is walled off, the air has nowhere to go except through the servers.

The result is that a machine at the top of a rack draws air at the same
temperature as one at the bottom. Servers that get warm start throttling, and
throttling is a common reason cheap hosting feels fast one minute and slow the
next.

![Cross-section of a contained cold aisle: chilled air enters the closed aisle between two rack rows, passes through the servers, and leaves as hot exhaust on the outside of each row.](/assets/static/datacenter/cold-aisle-en.svg)

*Containment is what keeps intake temperature even across the whole rack.*

## Network

The fibre into the building is redundant, and it reaches DE-CIX Frankfurt,
Speed-IX and Nikhef Amsterdam. Traffic to German and Dutch networks usually
stays regional instead of crossing the continent first.

Every server also sits behind aurologic's DDoS
protection, on every plan, at no extra
cost. Attack traffic is filtered upstream and never reaches your uplink.

![Network topology: Eygelshoven connects over two independent fibre paths each to Nikhef Amsterdam with Speed-IX, and to DE-CIX Frankfurt.](/assets/static/datacenter/network-map-en.svg)

*Every link is two independent fibre paths.*

## Security

Getting into the building is controlled and monitored. That part is easy, and on
its own it protects less than people assume.

So we lock the machines down too. Every host has a boot lock with TPM and BIOS
passwords, which means nobody can start poking at a server just by standing in
front of it. Every disk in our Ceph cluster is encrypted with LUKS2, so a drive
that leaves the building has nothing readable on it.

There is more on this in how secure is
Virtbase.
